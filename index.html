Sizing targets

One project-wide friction target drives duct size. With noise handled by the velocity maximum below, there is no reason for it to vary by room — and no minimum velocity on an air conditioning or ventilation system, because constant friction already picks the smallest duct that holds the target.

Pa/m

Flexible duct sizes on velocity (3.5 m/s, or the space's runout limit if lower), not on the target above, so the size doesn't swing with how compressed you assume it will be. This ceiling — on the compressed rate — is the backstop for small, long or crushed runs, where velocity alone would let the loss run away.

On a VSD or DCKV system, enter the minimum flow as a percentage of design and the velocity there is checked against 2.54 m/s (500 fpm) — NFPA 96 / IMC 506.3.4. That is the one velocity figure here which is a code requirement rather than guidance, and it is the reason a turndown system needs checking at its minimum, not at its design duty.

Dust / fume extract (LEV)

Sets the minimum transport velocity — ACGIH Industrial Ventilation design velocities, the lower bound of each published range. Below it the duct silts up. Unlike grease, this one really is about keeping material airborne. The transport velocity overrides the space's acoustic limit, because every dust class needs more than any space allows; the app says the duct will be loud rather than pretending otherwise. These figures came through secondary sources and should be checked against the ACGIH manual.
